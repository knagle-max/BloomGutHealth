# Bloom R4/M1 — Biology Content Quality Audit & Complete Profile Remediation

**Architect directive | Priority P0 user-facing quality within current R4/M1 RC | Date 2026-11-08**

**Owner-reported unacceptable patterns:**
- Bloodwork HDL: “HDL cholesterol is the cholesterol carried in high-density lipoprotein particles in the blood.” This gives no role, causes of low/high, risk interpretation, or practical context.
- Bloodwork homocysteine: “Homocysteine is an amino acid. This test measures its amount in the blood.” This omits B-vitamin processing, kidney/thyroid and medication context, interpretation limits, and what a person might discuss.
- A microbiome organism such as **Anaerobutyricum hallii** gets a useful cross-feeding/lactate/acetate/butyrate explanatory sentence in a list, then **“Research explanation not available yet”** when its detailed profile opens.
- Repeated “Reviewed research profile not yet available”, “No supported claim …”, “The name alone is not enough to classify it as beneficial or harmful”, “ask the provider”, “Search this organism in PubMed”, “What this record establishes”, “de-identified source page …” crowd out actual biology and research.

## 2. User experience to implement — meaningful concise first layer, precise deeper evidence
**Supported lab marker detail, target first screen (~100–170 plain-language words total across top-level sections, not a wall of text):**
- **What it does:** explain biologic function and *what lab measures*, not just dictionary label, 1–3 sentences.
- **Why low/high can happen:** concise, specific differential with common/high-yield contributors. If only one direction clinically meaningful, say so; **do not invent a harmful low end**.
- **Why it matters:** relevant risk/outcome associations, with observational vs causal limits and meaningful context with related labs/medications.
- **What to know or discuss:** 1–2 practical evidence-based actions or relevant follow-up tests/questions, not treatment instructions or target chasing, and not personalized diagnosis.
- Real source date, measured value, original units/assay, status, range, pending review, trend/source evidence preserved in clearly visible *Your results* module. Expand **Sources, studies, method, caveats and full marker data**. For unsupported source-only analytes: accurately display report/source and honest limited general meaning ONLY if identity is verified; do not silently assign clinical category, test range, or matched definition.

**Organism detail, target first screen (~90–160 words, excluding source observation):**
- **Meet this organism:** its usual ecology/habitat and concrete functions (fermentation, cross-feeding, substrate use, metabolites) WHEN evidence exists, at genus/species/strain scope accurately qualified.
- **Why researchers study it:** concrete findings (human association / mechanistic / culture / animal / interventional), with clear benefits and potential harms when literature supports both. Do not label a species inherently good/bad or assume strains share functions.
- **Food and metabolic connections:** only studied dietary substrates/paths; distinguish growth/abundance from metabolic function, not speculative personalized recipes or probiotics.
- **Your report:** name exactly as the provider wrote, taxonomy resolution, amount/status only if actually reported, original collection/report date and source. Distinguish “not reported” (specific field absent) from the fact the organism listing itself was reported: avoid nonsensical “Not reported · Reported observation.”
- **Deeper disclosure:** clickable studies (DOI/PubMed), evidence type, limits, method, reported taxonomy/source audit trail, conflicting results if any. Provider page/row provenance belongs here, not leading user takeaway. A literature link is a backup, **never a substitute for Bloom’s researched synopsis**.

**Consistency:** One canonical, versioned registry of scientific editorial content for each recognized marker/taxon, reused by list preview, search, favorites and profile detail. The profile can show more detail, but must **never be less informative** than its preview. Do not cache a stale fallback when source/name changes. Taxonomic aliases/synonyms explicitly versioned; distinguish `Anaerobutyricum hallii` and historical `Eubacterium hallii` and related `A. soehngenii` without assuming identical strain outcomes. Report raw source spelling and separately explain a reviewed synonym where proven.

## 4. Specific content prototypes for reviewer calibration (not blanket individualized advice)
**HDL cholesterol:** “HDL particles help move cholesterol from tissues toward the liver. HDL-C measures cholesterol carried in these particles, **not** how efficiently they function. Low HDL-C often travels with insulin resistance, higher triglycerides, smoking or inactivity, and is associated with higher cardiovascular risk. Very high HDL-C isn't automatically protective. Look at LDL-C, ApoB/non-HDL, triglycerides and overall risk together; medications that merely raise HDL-C have not reliably improved outcomes. Support cardiovascular health with activity, tobacco avoidance and dietary patterns that improve the overall lipid profile, rather than chasing HDL alone.” Sources: AHA `https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/cholesterol/about-cholesterol/what-your-cholesterol-levels-mean` plus source review for very-high-HDL claim before publication. **Do not frame HDL-C as the only cause/target.**

**Homocysteine:** “Homocysteine is formed while your body processes the amino acid methionine. Folate, B12 and B6 help recycle or break it down. Elevated results can reflect low B-vitamin status, reduced kidney function, hypothyroidism, medications or other factors. Higher levels have been linked to vascular problems, but lowering homocysteine with vitamins does **not** reliably prevent heart attacks or strokes in most people. If elevated, consider discussing B12/folate and kidney or thyroid results with a clinician rather than assuming an MTHFR variant is the cause.” Source: NIH MedlinePlus `https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/homocysteine-test/`. Avoid overinterpreting low levels without distinct evidence.

**Anaerobutyricum hallii:** “A gut bacterium studied for turning lactate and acetate made by neighboring microbes into butyrate, a short-chain fatty acid used by colon cells. This illustrates **cross-feeding**: one microbe's leftovers are another's ingredients. Laboratory demonstrations do not prove its butyrate-producing activity in an individual stool sample. Its current abundance, strain or benefit cannot be inferred from a name alone.” Research: `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21388423/` and `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15466518/` (historical Eubacterium hallii nomenclature; confirm synonym/taxonomy scope). Don't extrapolate related `A. soehngenii` human results to this exact species.

These are **initial writing benchmarks**, not proof every marker/organism is already rewritten or medically accepted. Any claim lacking verified source should be revised before shipping.
